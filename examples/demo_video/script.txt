The Boy Who Stayed Awake for 11 Days

[SFX soft room tone]

NARRATOR: In 1964, a high school student named Randy made a bet with his friends. He would try to break the world record for staying awake.

NARRATOR: No coffee tricks, no alarms, no help. Just a teenager, a calendar, and a very bad idea.

[SFX clock ticking]

NARRATOR: It started as a science fair project. For the first two days he felt completely fine. He played basketball outside, he joked with his friends, he felt like a superhero.

NARRATOR: But scientists at a nearby hospital heard about the attempt, and they decided to follow him around the clock. With clipboards. So many clipboards.

NARRATOR: On day three, the jokes stopped being funny. By day four, simple things became hard. He stood in front of the open refrigerator and forgot why he walked into the kitchen.

NARRATOR: On day five, his eyes stopped focusing properly. The world went slightly soft and blurry, like someone smeared grease on a camera lens.

NARRATOR: And then the strange things started. A road sign at the corner looked like a person standing very, very still. He knew it was a sign. He stared at it anyway.

NARRATOR: His mood fell apart. A friend made one small joke and Randy snapped at him like he had been stung. Sleep debt is not polite.

NARRATOR: The doctors tested his memory. Count backwards from one hundred by sevens, they said. He made it to sixty-five. Then he stopped, and could not remember what he was doing.

NARRATOR: Worse were the microsleeps. His eyes stayed open, but his brain quietly shut down for two or three seconds. He never knew when it would happen.

NARRATOR: In the lab, the brain wave monitor told the truth. Even while he was walking around wide awake, tiny fragments of sleep were sneaking into his brain behind his back.

NARRATOR: On day nine he picked up a newspaper and could not finish reading one single headline. For one scary moment, his own name felt unfamiliar.

NARRATOR: Then, on day eleven, two hundred and sixty-four hours after this whole thing started, he finally stopped. The record was broken.

[SFX cheering]

NARRATOR: When he finally went to bed, he slept for fourteen straight hours. And when he woke up, the doctors checked him from head to toe. No permanent damage at all.

NARRATOR: His body simply collected every hour of sleep it was owed, and moved on like nothing happened.

NARRATOR: But here is the part that should keep YOU up at night. After just one night without sleep, your reaction time drops like a phone with one percent battery.

NARRATOR: So if you are lying awake right now, scrolling on your phone in the dark, think of Randy and the road sign that looked like a person. Put the phone down.

NARRATOR: Because the record he set still stands today. Not because nobody could break it, but because after Randy, nobody was ever allowed to try again.

NARRATOR: Sweet dreams. Do not stare at any road signs.
