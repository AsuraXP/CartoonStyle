The 2:07 AM Glitch

[SFX soft room tone]

NARRATOR: Every night, the same thing happens. You wake up at exactly 2:07 AM, and the room feels wrong.

NARRATOR: The alarm clock glows red across the room. The window is dark. And for a few seconds, you cannot move.

NARRATOR: You tell yourself it is nothing. Just stress. Just a weird dream. But then you notice the shadows in the corner are a little darker than they should be.

NARRATOR: Your brain, half asleep, starts writing a story. Maybe it is a ghost. Maybe the house is haunted. Maybe you are still dreaming right now.

NARRATOR: So you do the only sensible thing. You pull the blanket up to your chin and stare at the ceiling like it owes you money.

[SFX clock ticking]

NARRATOR: Scientists actually have a name for this. It is called sleep paralysis, and it happens when your brain wakes up before your body does.

NARRATOR: Your mind is online. Your muscles are still offline. And in that tiny gap, your imagination gets the whole stage to itself.

NARRATOR: That is why the coat on the chair looks like a person. That is why the curtain moves when there is no wind. Your brain is filling in the blanks with its greatest hits of fear.

NARRATOR: The good news? It passes. In a minute or two, your body catches up, the shadows go back to being shadows, and you are just a person in a bed again.

NARRATOR: The bad news? Tomorrow night, at exactly 2:07 AM, the whole show runs again.

NARRATOR: So if you are awake right now, staring at your screen in the dark, do me a favor. Blink. Move your toes. Check the time.

NARRATOR: And whatever you do, do not look at the corner of the room.
